In Qeet ID, an **organization is the tenant** — the top-level container that
owns users, groups, roles and settings. Teams and workspaces within an
organization are modelled as **groups**.

## Organization = tenant

Each organization has a `slug`, `name`, `status`, `plan`, `region` and an open
JSONB metadata bag. Every other object (users, groups, roles, audit entries)
belongs to exactly one organization, which is how [multi-tenancy](/docs/concepts/tenancy)
isolation works.

## Groups (teams & workspaces)

Groups partition users within an organization and can be **nested** (a group can
have a parent), so you can model departments, teams and sub-teams. Groups are
also the unit of role inheritance and SCIM sync:

- Grant a role to a group and every member inherits it.
- SCIM keeps group membership in sync with a customer's directory.

## Memberships & roles

Membership is expressed through **role grants** rather than a separate members
table: a user belongs to an organization by holding one or more of its roles
(directly or via a group). See
[roles & permissions](/docs/authorization/roles-and-permissions).

## Invitations

Invite a user by email with a target role. Invitations carry a hashed token and
a status (`pending`, `accepted`, `revoked`, `expired`) and expire automatically.

```ts title="server.ts"
await qeetid.invitations.create({
  tenant: tenantId,
  email: "grace@example.com",
  role: "member",
});
```

## Branding & custom domains

Each organization can theme its hosted login and emails — logo, primary and
secondary colors, from-name/address and email templates — and claim a **custom
domain**:

- Domain claims are verified by **DNS TXT record**, and only one tenant may hold
  a verified claim for a given domain.
- A verified domain gates organization SSO and JIT provisioning.

<Callout type="warn">
  Qeet ID stores and DNS-verifies custom domains, but TLS certificates for them
  are issued at the deployment edge (the reverse proxy), not by the application.
  See [self-hosting](/docs/architecture/self-hosting).
</Callout>

## Switching organizations

A user who belongs to several organizations can switch the active one; the React
SDK ships an `<OrganizationSwitcher />` and a `useOrganization` hook, and the
backend verifies membership on every switch.
